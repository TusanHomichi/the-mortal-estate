## Decided by the owner (2026-09-05)

- **Surface geography stays the same as the selected source map.** Preserve its
  geographic layout as the design baseline. Do not replace it with an independently
  invented land or distort it merely to reduce recognition.
- **Structures show elapsed time.** Former buildings may be absent. Those that
  survive may be aged and overgrown. Preservation of surface geography does not
  require intact historical buildings.
- **The new town occupies the old town's footprint.** It is visibly newer and
  differently laid out within that footprint. Its internal arrangement is authored
  anew; the settlement's geographic placement remains fixed.
- **A new character arrives at the dock.** Walking from that spawn point toward
  town is the first recognition sequence. The approach should feel familiar to
  someone who remembers the source land, before the rebuilt town reveals how
  much has changed.

### Lodge gathering and locker room

**Owner-proposed direction (2026-09-06):** combine the arrival lodge with the
town's shared locker room and include a table where players can leave equipment
recovered from the dungeon that they believe belonged to another player who
died. The intended social role is a place to prepare, return, meet and help
other players recover their belongings. This supports social dependence and
world memory through repeated visits and voluntary acts of care.

**Proposed spatial treatment:** put the lockers, gearing benches and recovered
equipment table in the lodge's common room, near its main circulation and shared
seating. Arriving players should encounter the people who use the room. Keep
the table visible and distinct from dining furniture. A generous porch, worn
threshold, equipment racks and visible gathering space can communicate this
role under the [exterior-purpose direction](../presentation-direction.md#building-purpose-from-the-street).
Exact furnishings and room layout remain design candidates.

The table is proposed as a place for player-led returns. Item identification,
locker capacity and access, who may take deposited gear, persistence, and how
death or succession affects a claim remain open. Do not infer an automatic
return service or ownership protection from this spatial proposal. Future
mechanics belong to the [item-location owner](../boundary-map.md#17-one-item-one-location-one-transaction)
and the boundary map's death and lineage seams; this brief owns the building's
proposed role and arrangement.
