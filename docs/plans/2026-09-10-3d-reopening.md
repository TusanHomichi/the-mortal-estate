## Private study delivered

An external standalone browser study uses the actual entrance footprint and a
frozen observation mask from the earlier native dungeon capture: 49 display
cells, of which 26 were observed. It displays only those observed cells, with
placeholder masonry, hinged doors, stairs and the custom adventurer.
Camera elevation and yaw are adjustable. It does not compute sight or connect
to gameplay. Source code, asset hashes, the source-motion license, captures and
proof scripts are retained in its external packet.

The study transfers source motion using world bind-pose corrections and restores
the target's authored local transforms when changing clips. The initial reset
experiment exposed a root-scale collapse; restoring the authored transforms
fixed it, and proof now refuses a collapsed or stationary skeleton. This is
private experiment code, not a new production retargeting contract.

Native hardware proof passed Chromium, Firefox and WebKit: nine existing/trial
clips animate the custom 24-bone body, all three inspected elevations (40, 60,
75 degrees) retain the fixture, door toggles work, and browser errors/warnings
are absent. The first Firefox pass reported implementation-dependent filtering
for comparison-depth shadows; the study now selects basic nearest-depth shadows
consistently across browsers. Captures and a Chromium motion recording are
external. These checks prove technical playback, not animation acceptance.

texture rebuild or animation. The earlier animated version remains rejected

A delegated implementation corrects local-door movement under the

steps. The same evaluator supplies preview and commit. Browser proposal checks
observed open state without accepting a different endpoint silently. The change
is independent of the held presentation candidate and requires its own playable
verification and preview refresh.

The owner subsequently selected full black for unseen space and limited fixed
wall-torch lighting, superseding the fog veil and automatic player-light ideas.
Both fog implementations are inactive external history. The active study uses
provisional world-anchored torch positions, a short light radius, and architectural
shadow maps cached until the observed scene or door state changes. A small warm
bounce approximation accompanies the torches. Actor shadow casting is disabled
in this static-cache trial to avoid stale animated silhouettes; dynamic contact
shadows and final torch placements remain presentation work. This is not a
claim of finished lighting or continuous world rendering. The trial now culls
opposite-face torch fixtures even when their wall tile is observed. The complete shadow
pass is cached between scene/door changes, alongside per-light maps. A suspected
missing-actor rendering defect was rejected by direct pixel comparison: the
saved scene pixels were identical with the per-light and whole-pass caches.
Lighting ownership still needs to improve dark
character fronts and coarse stair shadows, with native entrance and doorway
captures required before visual acceptance.
